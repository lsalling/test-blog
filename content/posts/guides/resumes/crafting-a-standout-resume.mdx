Whether you're writing a resume with no experience or refining an established one, a standout resume is non-negotiable in today’s competitive professional world. It’s more than a list of jobs; it’s your chance to grab a hiring manager’s attention and prove you’re the right fit. This guide explains how to write a professional resume while highlighting what recruiters look for, including resume keywords and design details like font size.  

We’ll break down four essential rules for building a resume that shines. Each section blends core principles with actionable steps to help you succeed:  

* **Tailor for your audience:** Align your resume with the employer’s needs.  
* **Highlight impactful achievements:** Focus on results, not just duties.  
* **Polish with professional formatting:** Prioritize readability and ATS compatibility. 
* **Streamline for efficiency:** Keep your message concise and scannable.  

Whether you’re new to the workforce or a seasoned professional, these rules will help you craft a resume that stands out. Let’s get started.

## Chapter 1: Tailor for your audience  

A great resume starts with customization. Tailoring doesn’t mean rewriting everything for every job—it means aligning your story with what the employer values. If you’re writing a resume with no experience, emphasize transferable skills, coursework, internships, or volunteer work that relates to the role.  

### Why tailoring matters (and when to skip it)  
A tailored resume shows you’ve researched the role and understand the employer’s needs. It also improves your odds with Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) by including **resume keywords** from the job description. However, over-tailoring for every application can waste time, especially in fast-moving industries. Focus on creating a few targeted versions for different role types (e.g., marketing vs. IT) using a master document of your experiences.  

### Practical strategy for customization  
Scan job listings for common skills and prioritize those in your resume. For dream roles, mirror the company’s priorities, such as innovation or teamwork. For example, if a job emphasizes project management, highlight your experience coordinating teams or meeting deadlines. This ties into [Chapter 4’s focus on efficiency](#chapter-4-streamline-for-efficiency), where we’ll discuss balancing detail with brevity.  

### The payoff  
A tailored resume stands out by speaking directly to the employer’s needs. It’s a shortcut to relevance without burning you out.

## Chapter 2: Highlight impactful achievements  

Your resume isn’t a job description—it’s a highlight reel. Focus on achievements, not just tasks, to show what you bring to the table.  
### Why achievements matter  
Generic duties like “handled customer inquiries” don’t stand out. Instead, use specific results: “Resolved 95% of customer issues within 24 hours.” Numbers and impact grab attention quickly, which is critical when recruiters skim resumes in seconds.  
### How to write achievement statements  
Start with action verbs like “launched” or “improved,” then add the result. For example:  
- “Redesigned training program, increasing team productivity by 25%.”  
- “Guided a 12-person team through a company-wide software rollout.”  

If you lack hard metrics, emphasize scope or outcomes. Connect these achievements to the tailored skills mentioned in [Chapter 1](#chapter-1-tailor-for-your-audience) by choosing wins that align with your target role.  
### The edge it gives  
Achievement-focused resumes turn past successes into future potential. They also set up the next step: presenting these wins clearly through professional formatting.

## Chapter 3: Polish with professional formatting  

Content matters, but design ensures your resume gets read. A clean, consistent layout helps recruiters and ATS process your information quickly.  
### Key formatting basics  
- **Fonts:** Use ATS-safe options like Arial or Calibri.  
- **Font size:** 10–12 points for body text; 14–16 for headings.  
- **Consistency:** Stick to one font style and use uniform spacing.  
Avoid graphics or complex layouts—they often confuse ATS systems.  
### Industry-specific tweaks  
Designers might use bolder layouts, while finance professionals should prioritize text clarity. Free industry-specific templates can help balance style and function. Pair this with the achievement-focused content from [Chapter 2](#chapter-2-highlight-impactful-achievements) to ensure your wins stand out.  
### Why it works  
A polished format respects the recruiter’s time and ensures your achievements are easy to find.

## Chapter 4: Streamline for efficiency  

As we already mentioned in our previous and more general article about how resume writing works, recruiters will spend just around 7 full seconds looking at resume before they deem you worthy, making the polish and conciseness of your resume an extremely crucial part in scoring an interview with a company.
### Keep it concise  
- **Length:** Aim for one page if you have under 10 years of experience.  
- **Focus:** Trim older or irrelevant roles, emphasizing content tailored to the job (as discussed in [Chapter 1](#chapter-1-tailor-for-your-audience)).  
### Structure for speed  
- Use bullet points (4–5 per role) for achievements.  
- Example: “Cut costs by 15% with new vendor process.”  
- Follow the formatting tips from [Chapter 3](#chapter-3-polish-with-professional-formatting) to maintain scannability.
